## 1. Context

The TalentHub product moved from a labor-oriented social network into an assisted application platform with three approved pillars (explainable compatibility, evidence-backed profile, assisted application). The four-persona restructuring (Student, Company, School, External) is integrated into `main` and lives on `stabilization/release-readiness` for the release-readiness mission. The current canonical state is:

- One `profiles` table for persons, with `account_type` and `account_status` as the canonical columns (`supabase/migrations/20260726000007_canonical_identity_access.sql:15-46`).
- One wide `schools` table for institutions with a back-reference to a school owner's `profile_id` (lines 52-62, 78-85). No generic `organizations` table. **`schools.profile_id` is a physical legacy column in the `schools` adapter table** (rev. 4.2 — supersedes the prior "profile_id legacy adapter / backfill column" framing); the canonical owner reference is the membership row (D-07). **The canonical `organizations` row has no `profile_id` column** (rev. 4.2).

## 3. Alternatives considered (mandate A–K)

The mandate records eleven structural alternatives. They are recorded here for traceability; the decision is not adopted in this ADR.

- **A. Organizations as a single generic table with `kind`.** Adopted in §7 (target model). The canonical shape is **`organizations(id, kind, legal_name, display_name, slug, description, logo_url, status, created_at, updated_at, …)` with `kind IN ('school', 'company')` and no `profile_id` column** (REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING; supersedes the prior "profile_id adapter / backfill" framing). `schools` and `company_profiles` are physical adapter tables during the transition (D-42, D-07). The canonical owner reference is the `organization_memberships.member_role = 'owner'` row (D-07); the human relationship lives **only** in `organization_memberships`. The physical legacy columns `schools.profile_id` and `company_profiles.profile_id` are **physical legacy columns in the adapter tables**; they are not introduced as canonical on the `organizations` row. **No future policy reads `organizations.profile_id`.**
- **B. Organizations as two parallel tables (`schools`, `companies`).** Rejected by the mandate: D-03 + D-04 require a single `kind` discriminator.
- **C. Organizations as profiles (a flat list of persons and institutions).** Rejected by the mandate: D-01 forbids organizations in `profiles`; D-02 forbids organizations from authenticating.
- **D. Memberships as a single generic table (`organization_memberships`).** Adopted in §7 (D-07). `school_members` becomes a view (D-42) **after** the inbound FKs have moved.
- **E. Memberships as a JSON column on `organizations`.** Rejected by the mandate: D-07 requires an explicit membership table.
- **F. Memberships on the resource ownership column (publisher/applicant only).** Rejected by the mandate: D-07 requires an authority layer that is independent of resource ownership; D-40 makes the two concepts distinct.
- **G. Resource ownership as a single `created_by_profile_id` column.** Rejected for ownership; **adopted for actor audit (D-39)**. The mandate's "ownership and creator are distinct" (D-40) means the resource ownership column is a separate column (`publisher_organization_id` for company opportunities only — D-30: school is not a publisher of `opportunities` of any type — and `publisher_profile_id` for external personal opportunities), not `created_by_profile_id`.
- **H. Resource ownership as `actor_id` on an events table only.** Rejected by the mandate: D-39 requires the actor to be recorded on the resource itself, not on the timeline.
- **I. Single opportunity model (ADR-003) with type discriminator.** Adopted in §7 (D-26). `opportunity_type` is the discriminator.
- **J. Two opportunity models (corporate + freelance) with separate publication lifecycle.** Rejected by the mandate: D-26 requires a single canonical model.
- **K. Two opportunity models (corporate + freelance) with a shared lifecycle module.** Recorded as alternative D in the gap analysis §8.4. **Not adopted** in this ADR; it is a deferred decision (D-OD-1) and is **not** a gate for this ADR's acceptance.

### 7.3 Resource ownership — XOR invariant (D-31, D-32, D-33, D-40)

- For every resource with an organizational vs personal owner, the invariant is `(*_organization_id IS NOT NULL) <> (*_profile_id IS NOT NULL)`. Both null and both set are forbidden. The XOR table is recorded in the gap analysis §7.4 with the actor, owner, context and per-resource rule. The ADR records the invariant; the gap analysis records the per-resource application.
- **Posts: actor vs owner (rev. 4).** For `posts`, the actor is `posts.author_profile_id` (FK → `profiles.id`, **NOT NULL, always required, NOT in the XOR**). The ownership XOR is on `posts.organization_id` (nullable): `NULL` = personal post (the author is the personal owner), set = organizational post (the organization is the owner; the author is the actor, membership-gated). `author_profile_id` does **not** participate in the XOR; it is the actor, always required.
- **Opportunities: school is NOT a publisher organizacional (D-30).** The owner-invariant in the abstract supports `organizations.kind IN ('company', 'school')` as the published-by side of the XOR. However, the canonical resource `opportunities` (D-26, D-30) accepts **only `kind = 'company'`** as the publisher organizacional; **`kind = 'school'` does not publish `opportunities` of any type** (`job`, `internship`, `company_project`, `freelance_request`). `opportunities.publisher_organization_id` is therefore set **only** for `kind = 'company'`. Any mention of a `school` organization in this ADR is contextual (school ownership of other resources, school mediation of contact requests, school validation of evidence) and is never a publisher of `opportunities`.
- **Contact requests and contact authorizations (D-37, D-38, rev. 4).** `contact_requests.requester_profile_id XOR requester_organization_id` expresses who initiates and owns the request. `student_contact_authorizations` follows the same XOR (`organization_id` XOR `profile_id`). `school_id` (the school organization) is **mediator context** (D-37, D-38) and is **never** an implicit owner. Modify / delete semantics: the requester (the organization owner or the personal owner) can cancel / update; school is **mediator** (`school_id` context) and never owner. Membership revocation: if the actor of the requester loses membership (`status` transitions to `suspended` or `disabled`; rev. 4 — the token `revoked` is not a status), the request is not deleted; the requester remains the logical owner until cancel / expire; future edits by the original actor are denied by the RLS layer.
- The client does not provide either FK (D-31, D-32, D-33). The server action derives the set column from the session and the membership. The set column is the canonical authority for the ownership question; the RLS layer reads the set column, not the `account_type` of the actor.
- The author (the human who pressed the button, recorded in `created_by_profile_id` for audit, D-39) is **not** the owner. The author and the owner are distinct concepts (D-40). The membership is the bridge between the author and the organization (D-07). When a member's `status` transitions to `suspended` or `disabled` (the canonical non-active states; rev. 4), the resources the member authored are **not** deleted; the owner is still the organization; future edits by the non-active member are denied; future edits by other members of the same organization are still allowed. The Company A / membership-`disabled` example is recorded in the gap analysis §10.2.

### 7.7 Views, grants and security (D-43, §13.4 of the gap analysis)

- The phrase **"security_definer view"** is **not** a category in this document (REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING). A view is either `security_invoker = true` or it runs with the implicit default. The only `security_definer` objects in this codebase are **functions**; the only "owner-behavior" views are documented as such, with the constraints in (ii) below. The three real alternatives (REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING) are:
  - **(i) `security_invoker = true` view** with minimum grants on the view and on the base tables, column allowlist on the view and the base, and RLS. "Minimum column-level grants" does not mean broad grants: `PUBLIC` and `anon` receive no base-table grants, and `authenticated` receives only the explicitly required columns when an authenticated invoker view needs them. Used for the authenticated projection list and for internal compatibility views by default.
  - **(ii) View with owner behavior** (a dedicated owner role, no `BYPASSRLS`, allowlist, minimum grants, **not** mutable by application users). Used internally where justified. This is **not** called a "security_definer view" in this document; the dedicated owner role is the security boundary.
  - **(iii) `SECURITY DEFINER` FUNCTION** (REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING) with fixed `search_path`, allowlisted structured output, minimum `EXECUTE` and audit. Used for the **public** projection pattern. The public surface is the function, not a view; **no `SELECT` grants on a view for the public rows**.

Not labelled a "security_definer view".
  - **Authenticated projections** = `security_invoker = true` view with minimum grants on the view and only the required column-level grants on the base, column allowlist, RLS. `PUBLIC` and `anon` receive no base grants.

- **I-18.** **Unique active owner (REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING — 8-step sequence, at-most-one / at-least-one / exactly-one, status vocabulary, no ordinary operations in pending / suspended / recovery).** For `organizations.status = 'active'`, **exactly one membership has `member_role = 'owner'` and `status = 'active'`** (D-05). The invariant is the combination of **at-most-one** (no two active owners) and **at-least-one** (every active organization has at least one active owner, with brief exceptions during the transfer window and the controlled recovery); the combination is **exactly-one**. The initial UI exposes only one owner. The transfer is an **8-step conceptual sequence (no SQL)**; it is an **atomic** operation that must not be partially observable: an external observer must never see a state with two active owners, a state with zero active owners, or a state in which the new owner is not yet `active`. The conceptual sequence is: (1) **lock** the organization and its memberships (row-level lock); (2) **verify current** owner membership exists with `member_role = 'owner'`, `status = 'active'`; (3) **verify successor** identity and active membership; (4) **create or activate the successor's membership without `member_role = 'owner'`** (a non-owner role, e.g. `admin` or `teacher`, with `status = 'active'`); (5) **neutralize the previous owner in the same transaction** (set `member_role = 'admin'`, `status = 'active'` — the previous owner retains `active` status and becomes `admin`; they do not lose access; the neutralization is the physical step that resolves the unique-index constraint); (6) **promote the successor in the same transaction** (set `member_role = 'owner'`, `status = 'active'`; the promotion happens physically after the neutralization so that, at every intermediate write, the partial unique index sees at most one `active` `owner` row); (7) **register the audit event** (insert the transfer event into the audit log with `created_by_profile_id = previous_owner`; the audit records the historical actor; it does not record ownership); (8) **verify exactly one active owner before commit** (within the same transaction, count the `active` `owner` rows for the organization; the count must be exactly 1; if it is 0 or > 1, raise an exception and roll back; only then commit). The implementation must take the appropriate lock and coordinate uniqueness / state so the unique-index invariant is not violated and no committed state is left in a non-invariant form. The unique partial index that enforces "at most one active owner per organization" (e.g. `UNIQUE (organization_id) WHERE member_role = 'owner' AND status = 'active'`) is **not deferrable** in PostgreSQL; therefore "defer the index" is **not** a valid implementation option. The physical order (neutralize then promote) is the opposite of the logical flow (transfer and then demote) and is required because the unique partial index is **not deferrable** in PostgreSQL and the previous owner must be removed from the partial-index set before the new owner is added. **No commit intermediate without an owner.** **No SQL is chosen in this ADR.** The membership status contract is `active | invited | suspended | disabled`; the token `revoked` is **not** a status. **Pending / suspended / recovery states have no ordinary operations**: no automatic transitions, no auto-promotion; suspending the owner membership does **not** auto-promote a different member; the organization becomes **ownerless** and the only way to leave the ownerless state is a service-role transfer (Gate D concern). **Revocation that leaves the organization ownerless is forbidden** by the invariant except as part of a controlled exceptional recovery (service-role action, audit-logged, with a designated successor). The implementation (partial unique index, transactional function, defensive trigger, or combination) is a Gate B2 decision, not an ADR decision. **The 7-step framing in rev. 4 / rev. 4.1 is HISTORICAL — SUPERSEDED BY REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING.**

The Company A / membership revoked example (gap analysis §10.2) demonstrates the principle: the column records the historical actor; it does not grant or revoke any capability.
- **I-20.** **Views, grants and security principles (rev. 4.2 — three real alternatives; no "security_definer view"; public projections = `SECURITY DEFINER` functions; `security_barrier` only in views where applicable, not in functions; runtime grants are Gate D).** Every view, helper and grant introduced by the migration follows the principles in the gap analysis §13.4: allowlist over blocklist; no `SELECT *`; `security_barrier` **only in views where applicable**, **not in functions** (rev. 4.2). The public projection table is **exactly 6 rows** (rev. 4.2). The **6 public rows are produced through `SECURITY DEFINER` functions** (the 6 public projections expose **function output and `EXECUTE`**, **not "view grants"** — the public surface is the function, not a view; rev. 4.2). Internal IDs as foreign keys (`profile_id`, `organization_id`) are not in any public projection. **Authenticated projections = `security_invoker = true` view + minimum grants on view and base + RLS** (rev. 4.2). **Internal compatibility views = `security_invoker = true` view by default** (rev. 4.2); owner-behavior or `SECURITY DEFINER` only when justified in the migration plan. The historical wording "per-row coherent `security_definer` exception for the 6 public projections" (rev. 4 / rev. 4.1) is **HISTORICAL — SUPERSEDED BY REV 4.2**: the canonical state is that public projections are `SECURITY DEFINER` functions (not "security_definer views"). The previous "6 public rows use the controlled `security_definer` exception" is replaced by "6 public rows are produced through `SECURITY DEFINER` functions". ### 11.12 Expand / transition / contract — framework

Every step in the migration plan is structured as **expand / transition / contract** (full framework in gap analysis §15.12). The framework is independent of the specific DDL; the migration plan implements it. The principles are:

- **Expand** is additive and idempotent; the legacy structure continues to work; the new structure is created alongside.
- **Transition** is gradual, observable and centralized; a single code path at a time; the legacy structure is read-only after the dual-write window ends.
- **Contract** is forward-only non-destructive; the legacy structure is removed only when the new structure has zero legacy consumers; the new structure's ids are preserved.
- **No duplicates, safe re-run, checksums when viable, rejected rows recorded, no overwrite of new data, verifiers and reconciliation, centralized dual-write layer, adapters, contract only at the end.**

The freelance rename (step 5) applies the framework with the specifics in gap analysis §15.13: the contract is forward-only non-destructive; the `opportunity_id` is preserved; the `opportunity_proposals` table is **not** renamed; the counts before and after each phase are recorded.

## 12. Rollback plan

The rollback plan is the inverse of §11. Each step is independently reversible because the migration is **additive** during the verification window. The following holds for every step:

- The `organizations` and `organization_memberships` tables are additive. Dropping them restores the pre-migration state. The `schools` and `school_members` views are dropped and the original tables are restored from a backup.
- The `created_by_profile_id` column is additive during the backfill window. Dropping the column restores the pre-migration state. The NOT NULL constraint is part of the same migration; the rollback drops the column.
- The `opportunity_type` rename is an expand/contract migration. The rollback for the expand is to re-add `freelance` to the CHECK; the rollback for the contract is to re-add `freelance` and revert the application code.
- The `student_enrollments` table is additive (REV 4.2 FINAL DOCUMENTARY CLEANUP / AUDIT PENDING: live + history). Rollback does **not** delete `student_enrollments` as the sole restoration action: its history rows, mappings, adapters and reads are preserved or restored. If a reversal requires hiding the structure, the history remains in a verifiable copy or mapping and the structure is restored without loss. The rollback is conceptual here; executable detail belongs to the migration plan under B2/D.
- The notifications minor scope is a **payload / link redaction** and a **no-new-channel policy** (D-OD-7). The rollback is to revert the redaction and the policy.
- The `role` retirement is a predicate rewrite. The inverse is restoring the original predicates from a backup.

**Conceptual rollback of adapters and views (rev. 4.2).**

- **During expand.** The physical legacy tables (`schools`, `company_profiles`, `school_members`) are **not** dropped. The mappings (legacy `schools.id` / `company_profiles.profile_id` → canonical `organizations.id`) are preserved and verifiable. The adapters (e.g. the read-through that keeps `student_profiles.school_id` in sync with `student_enrollments`) are active. A backup of the pre-expand state is taken and verifiable.
- **During transition.** The adapters may be read-only; the canonical structure is being written by the application layer. The mappings are stable; the legacy ids are not re-mapped. The verification scripts confirm the count and the checksum.
- **Adapter retirement.** The adapters (e.g. the read-through for `student_profiles.school_id`; the physical `schools` table if it has become a view) may be **retired only after** consumers, FKs, policies and RLS predicates have been migrated and verified. The retirement is forward-only non-destructive.
- **Rollback (revert to expand).** If the transition is rolled back, the **adapter physical table is restored** (the table is dropped-as-view and the physical table is restored from a backup, or the adapter is reintroduced), the **mappings are restored** (the legacy ids are restored to the canonical mapping), the **previous reads are restored** (the application layer reads through the adapter again), and the **history of enrollments is preserved** (no row of `student_enrollments` is lost in the rollback; the history is part of the rollback surface). The rollback is conceptual, not a SQL script; the plan does not assert SQL; the rollback is a description of the state to restore and the surface to verify (mappings, backups, reads, history of enrollments). The actual rollback DDL is the migration plan's responsibility (Gate B2 / D).

The rollback plan is **not** an excuse to skip the staged migration. Every step is verified with the `verify:*` script chain before the next step is started.

### 16.5 Ownership — 8-step conceptual sequence, exactly-one (residue 4)

- **Conceptual 8-step sequence (rev. 4.2 cleanup, canonical; supersedes the prior transfer framing).** (1) block the organization and its memberships (row-level lock); (2) verify the current owner; (3) verify the successor's identity and active membership; (4) create or activate the successor's membership **without** `member_role = 'owner'`; (5) neutralize the previous owner in the same transaction; (6) promote the successor to `member_role = 'owner', status = 'active'` in the same transaction; (7) register the audit event; (8) verify exactly one active owner **before commit**.
- **Three derived rules.**
  - **At-most-one.** No two active `owner` memberships on the same organization. The unique partial index enforces this at the database; "defer the index" is not a valid implementation option (the index is not deferrable in PostgreSQL).
  - **At-least-one.** Every active organization has at least one active `owner` membership, with brief exceptions during the transfer window and the controlled recovery.
  - **Exactly-one.** The combination: for `organizations.status = 'active'`, exactly one membership with `member_role = 'owner'` and `status = 'active'`. **Pending / suspended / recovery states have no ordinary operations** (no automatic transitions, no auto-promotion).
- **Atomicity.** No two active owners (no partial state with two). No active organization without an owner (no partial state with zero). No auto-promotion on suspension. Revocation requires **prior transfer** or **organizational suspension**; the controlled recovery path is a service-role action recorded in the audit log.
- **In-place corrections to this ADR.** §7.2 (Organizations) and §8 I-18 (Unique active owner) and the gap analysis §5.4 are the canonical references. The former transfer-sequence residue is **HISTORICAL — SUPERSEDED BY REV 4.2**. The 8-step sequence is the canonical. The "promote before demote" wording is not valid against a non-deferrable partial unique index; the canonical physical order is neutralize-then-promote. No new clause is added; the rev. 4.2 wording is reaffirmed with the cleanup tag.

### 17.6 Ownership — 8-step conceptual sequence, exactly-one (residue 4) — in-place corrected

- **Three derived rules (canonical).** **At-most-one** (no two active owners), **at-least-one** (every active organization has at least one active owner, with brief exceptions during the transfer window and the controlled recovery), **exactly-one** (the combination; for `organizations.status = 'active'`, exactly one membership with `member_role = 'owner'` and `status = 'active'`).
- **Pending / suspended / recovery states have no ordinary operations** (no automatic transitions, no auto-promotion). **Atomicity**: no two active owners, no active organization without an owner, no auto-promotion on suspension. Revocation requires **prior transfer** or **organizational suspension**.
- **In-place corrections to this ADR (canonical).** §8 I-18, §7.2, §15.A.4, §15.A.10, §11 Step 1, the gap analysis §5.4 and §19 are the canonical references. The former transfer-sequence residue in I-18 is replaced; the historical wording is marked **HISTORICAL — SUPERSEDED BY REV 4.2**.
